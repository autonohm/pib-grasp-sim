\subsection{Learning Grasps for a Low-Cost Servo Hand}

pib's 3D-printed hand has eight bus servos and force sensors in the fingertips, but no camera or torque sensing. We train a
grasp policy that uses only joint angles and fingertip forces with PPO in Isaac Lab~\cite{mittal2025isaaclab} on a digital twin
of the hand, randomizing object shape, mass and friction. Cheap servos wear out when they jitter and shut down when they push
against their torque limit for too long. We therefore smooth the policy with CAPS~\cite{mysore2021caps} and limit the time
servos spend near (${\geq}\,80\,\%$) their stall torque with a PID Lagrangian~\cite{stooke2020pid}. In simulation (five
seeds), the policy holds 83\,\% of the benchmark objects (scripted grasp: 69\,\%) and 74--82\,\% of unseen YCB
objects~\cite{calli2015ycb}, while servos spend 13\,\% instead of 32\,\% of the time near stall. Code and all trained policies
are open source\footnote{\url{https://github.com/churro206/pib-grasp-sim}}; tests on the real hand are pending.
